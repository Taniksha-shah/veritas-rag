A 64-year-old man developed lactic acidosis and hypoglycaemia during treatment with vildagliptin/metformin for type 2 diabetes mellitus (T2DM).

The man had a history T2DM, diabetic kidney disease, neuropathy, hyperuricaemia, insomnia and alcoholic liver disease and had been receiving vildagliptin/metformin [metformin hydrochloride] 50/250mg tablet twice daily [route not stated], pregabalin, tramadol [tramadol hydrochloride], paracetamol [acetaminophen], febuxostat and brotizolam. During COVID-19 pandemic, he spent most of his time at home by consuming alcohol during the day. Three days before the presentation, he had an extremely low food intake but continued alcohol consumption while taking his medications. Subsequently, he was found to be in a confused state by his family and was admitted to the emergency department. During admission, his vital signs included BP 128/77mm Hg, heart rate 102 beats per minute, RR 20 breaths per minute and oxygen saturation 98% and body temperature of 34.5°C. Based on a glucometer findings, he was diagnosed with hypoglycaemia secondary to vildagliptin/metformin.

The man was treated with glucose [dextrose]. Later, his lab parameters indicated renal insufficiency, electrolyte abnormalities, hepatic insufficiency and high anion gap metabolic acidosis with hyperlactataemia and β-hydroxybutyrate dominant hyperketonaemia. An ultrasound cardiograph revealed collapse of the inferior vena cava suggesting intravascular dehydration. Eventually, his consciousness improved after taking glucose. However, his systolic blood pressure decreased due to dehydration and severe metabolic acidosis. He was initiated on extracellular fluid resuscitation, vitamin B1 and sodium bicarbonate. Thereafter, his systolic blood pressure recovered. Fifteen hours later, his metabolic acidosis and hyperlactataemia improved. After one day of hospitalisation, he was initiated on phosphorous and vitamin B1 for prophylaxis of refeeding syndrome. His vildagliptin/metformin was discontinued. Further, his glycemic levels were well controlled and maintained under linagliptin and he was discharged after 13 days of hospitalisation and advised to stop alcohol consumption.

PRAC meeting highlights January 2022

Highlights of the Pharmacovigilance Risk Assessment Committee (PRAC) meeting held on 10−13 January 2022 included the initiation of a review of terlipressin-related safety concerns, and updates on transverse myelitis after the AstraZeneca COVID-19 vaccine [Vaxzevria; AZD-1222] and Janssen COVID-19 vaccine [Ad26.COV2-S], cases of thrombosis with thrombocytopenia syndrome (TTS) after Vaxzevria, and the risk of liver injury with cladribine [Mavenclad], reports the European Medicines Agency (EMA).

EMA's safety committee, PRAC, initiated a review of terlipressin-containing medicines after a request from the Danish medicines agency because of safety concerns about data from a large clinical trial in patients with hepatorenal syndrome. Results suggested that terlipressin-treated patients were more likely to die from respiratory disorders within 90 days after the first dose than those given placebo, and the frequency of respiratory failure (10%) was higher than reported in the product information (≤1%).

The PRAC has recommended updating product information for AstraZeneca and Janssen COVID-19 vaccines to include a warning of very rare cases of transverse myelitis after vaccination, and adding transverse myelitis as an adverse reaction of unknown frequency, after reviewing globally reported cases including those reported to EudraVigilance.

PRAC has also recommended updating product information for Vaxzevria to add more information about very rare cases of TTS after vaccination. Out of 1809 cases of TTS reported worldwide, 1643 were reported after the first dose and 166 after the second dose. Current product information advises that administration of a second dose of Vaxzevria is contraindicated if TTS is experienced after the first dose.

At the meeting, the PRAC also discussed a direct healthcare professional communication (DHPC) on the risk of liver injury with Mavenclad, with new recommendations on liver function monitoring. Liver injury will be included in the product information of Mavenclad as an adverse drug reaction of uncommon frequency. Each patient's history of liver disorders and liver injury should be reviewed before treatment initiation, and liver function tests should be repeated during treatment. The DHPC for Mavenclad will be forwarded to the EMA Committee for Medicinal Products for Human Use (CHMP) for a decision before it is disseminated to healthcare professionals and published in EU Member States.

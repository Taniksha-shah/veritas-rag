Introduction: ABO blood group system discovered by Karl Landsteiner in 1900s is the most important system for transfusion medicine. Four main blood groups in ABO system are A/ B/ AB/ O. ABO genes polymorphism may lead to reduced amounts of A/ B antigens and gives rise to subgroups of A/ B, which pose an enigmatic problem for immunohematologists. Blood group A is divided in A1/ A2-subgroups with A1 being more common. A1-lectin from Dolichos biflorus seeds reacts and agglutinates A1-cells but not A2-cells. As frequency of A1 and A2 for our region is not available, so this study was undertaken.

Aims and Objectives: To determine:

Frequency of A1 among blood group A and ABFrequency of A2 among blood group A and ABDifference in frequency of A1/ A2 among blood group A and AB.

Frequency of A1 among blood group A and AB

Frequency of A2 among blood group A and AB

Difference in frequency of A1/ A2 among blood group A and AB.

Materials and Methods: This study was conducted on accepted blood donors in Blood Bank of a teaching institute after getting ethical approval. Donor blood grouping was performed by conventional tube-technique. A1-lectin and H-lectin testing was done in A, AB and O-blood groups and classified as A1/A1B, A2/A2B and other A-subgroups and their frequencies determined. OP6

Immunohematological discrepancies – Due to nuisance antibody!

Ruhi Mehra, Savita Pujare, Shubhangi Saraswat, Nitin Chavan

LHRC, Mumbai, Maharashtra, India

Timely provision of blood and blood products plays an important role in the management algorithm of patients. Any delay can lead to detrimental effects by disrupting the cascade of events arranged in their treatment protocols. In elective planned major surgeries the Transfusion Medicine Department usually receives the patients sample for blood grouping and cross-matching at-least a day prior. The significance of such hospital protocols becomes evident whenever erratic testing results are obtained which require time for resolution. Even though interference by minor blood group antibodies has become a common entity amongst multiply transfused patients, other non-significant antibodies are too encountered rarely. These may be directed against potentiators, reagents, chemicals and preservatives in reagents thus often leading to confusion in the testing results, ultimately causing a delay in the provision of blood products. We report one such interfering non-specific antibody in a 55 year old female patient posted for splenectomy at our institute.

Blood group discrepancy with a pan-positive (4+) antibody screening panel (Bio-rad Diamed GmBH, Switzerland) was found in her testing results. The direct and indirect anti-globulin test (performed using in-house donor pooled cells) and auto-control was negative; Cross-matches were compatible at anti-human globulin phase and at room temperature. The possibility of drug-induced antibody was ruled out from medication history. Detailed meticulous immuno-hematological work-up helped us in ruling out that the offending antibody was not directed against the ingredients of column matrix of gel cards or the enhancement media (LISS); rather it was directed against the chemical constituents in the suspension medium of panel cells.

Non-specific “nuisance” antibodies create lot confusion and delay in the timely management of the patients thus leading to wastage of precious resources in a developing country like ours. Bearing their presence in mind becomes extremely important so that they can be detected and managed quickly.

Estimation of serum potassium and sodium levels in stored blood – A prospective study

Sunil Singh Narwaria, P D Sarkar, Rajeev Lohokare, Vandana Varma, Shiv Narayan Lahariya, Bhavna Tiwari

Department of Biochemistry, M.G.M. Medical College, Indore, Madhya Pradesh, India

Introduction: Potassium is principal intracellular cation and Sodium is principal extracellular cation. Maintenance of distribution of sodium and potassium between intracellular and extracellular compartments relies on several homeostasis mechanisms. Biochemical variations take place during storage of blood for transfusion. It has been observed that Hyperkalemia and Hyponatremia following transfusion of stored blood. During blood storage, leakage of potassium from cells in to plasma due to sodium-potassium ATPase pump failure.

Materials and Methods: The present study was conducted in the Department of Biochemistry and Department of I.H.B.T. (Transfusion Medicine) of M.G.M. Medical College and M.Y. Hospital Indore after approval from ethical committee in 20 randomly selected donar blood units in citrate phosphate dextrose adenine anticoagulant. Estimation of serum Sodium and Potassium was done by using Ion Selective Electrode technology. Biochemical changes were evaluated for 35 days in stored blood.

Results: Mean Potassium level is significantly increased from 7.12 mmol/L at baseline to 20.35 mmol/L at 5 weeks (P < 0.0001) and mean Sodium is significantly decreased from 150.23 mmol/L at baseline to 121.50 mmol/L at 5 weeks (P < 0.0001).

Conclusion: Potassium concentration significantly increased and Sodium concentration significantly decreased since first week of blood storage. Therefore aged blood should be avoided to prevent potential adverse outcomes from biochemical changes in stored blood. Biochemical changes in stored blood should also be tested before transfusion.

Keywords: Potassium, sodium, stored blood, transfusion

To compare the changes in various parameters such as Blood pressure (B.P.) and Heart rate (H.R.) among the whole blood donors before and 10 min after donation.

Materials and Methods: A prospective comparison study was conducted in the Department of Transfusion Medicine and Blood Bank, AIIMS, Raipur, for seven months (November 2021–June 2022) to evaluate the incidence of V.V.R. and to know the variations in vital parameters among two intervention groups.

Study Group: First-time donors of the age group 18–45 years who were eligible for blood donation according to CDSCO G.S.R. 166 (E) criteria.

OP12

Importance of antibody screening and identification in donors and general patients at a tertiary care hospital

Abhinav Verma

Max Super Speciality Hospital Vaishali, Ghaziabad, Uttar Pradesh, India

Introduction: Antibody screening is designed to detect clinically significant antibodies including those associated with hemolytic disease of the fetus and newborn (HDFN), hemolytic transfusion reactions or notably decreased survival of transfused red cells. When unexpected antibodies are present, as indicated by positive antibody screening tests, they must be identified.

Aims: The main aim of this study is to highlight the importance of antibody screening combined with phenotyping and identification in all transfused patients and blood donors.

Materials and Methods: The study was conducted in the Department of Transfusion Medicine for a period of 5 years from January 2017 to December 2021. Patients who need blood transfusion and blood donor samples were processed for ABO and Rh “D” grouping as well as antibody screening with 3 cell screening panel on fully automated immunohaematology analyzer.

Results: A total of 14,809 patients samples and 24,613 donor samples were processed for antibody screening out of which 107 patients (0.72%) and 9 donors (0.04%) were found to be positive for antibody screening. In 36 patients and 3 donors only autoantibodies were identified leading to 0.48% alloimmunization among patients. Out of 71 alloantibodies among patients most common antibodies were among the Rh blood group system Anti-D (24, 33.8%), Anti-E (13, 18.3%), Anti- C (7, 9.9%), Anti-C (4, 5.6%) and Anti-Kell (3, 4.2%). Out of 6 alloantibodies among donors most common antibodies was among the Rh blood group system Anti-D (3, 50%). In one donor alloantibody could not be identified.

Conclusions: Antibody screening combined with phenotyping and identification should be done in all transfused patients and blood donors. In our study antibodies against Rh and Kell blood group antigens were the most frequent amongst donors and general patients population requiring blood. So we suggest that at least Rh and Kell matched blood should be given to all patients for safe blood transfusion.

Changing trend in sero-prevalence of infectious markers amongst blood donors in a tertiary care ESI hospital in Delhi: A 5 year study

Bharat Singh, Hanisha Jain, Anita Mittal

ESI Model Hospital and PGIMSR, Delhi, India

Introduction: Blood transfusion is a life saving procedure however major problem associated with it is risk of transfusion transmitted infections (TTIs). The prevalence of TTIs varies from 0.2 % to 0.4% for HIV and 1%–10% for Hep B and Hep C, depending on geographical area of our country.

Aims and Objectives:

To determine the percentage of voluntary donors and replacement donorsTo estimate and compare the sero-prevalence and changing trends of TTIs amongst voluntary and replacement donors.

To determine the percentage of voluntary donors and replacement donors

To estimate and compare the sero-prevalence and changing trends of TTIs amongst voluntary and replacement donors.

Materials and Methods: This is a retrospective study carried out in the Centre, over a period of 5 years (2017-2021). The blood sample of each donor was screened for HIV I and II, Hepatitis B and Hepatitis C using 4th generation kits from M/S Bio-Rads India by ELISA technique. The demographic details and TTI test results of all donors were recorded. The malaria and syphilis test results were not analyzed as positivity was found in very few cases. The data was entered in MS excel sheet and statistics were calculated.

Results: Out of 28816 donors, 14955 (51.90%) were voluntary and 13823 (48.10) were replacement donors. We observed a steady increase in the number of voluntary donors as it was 21.5% in 2017 and reached 71.9% in 2021. The overall sero-prevalence of HIV I and II, Hep B and Hep C was higher in replacement donors (2.75%) as compared to voluntary donors (1.13%). Moreover, a decline in the overall sero-positivity was also observed from 2017 to 2021 as it decreased from 2.12% to 1.28%.

Conclusion: The increase in public awareness regarding voluntary blood donation has led to increasing trend of voluntary blood donation in the last five years. Voluntary blood donations, meticulous donor screening, counseling and use of highly sensitive methods for testing can make blood transfusion more safe till the NAT screening is made mandatory.

A comparative study of serum hepcidin levels in beta thalassemia major patients with healthy individuals in central India

Vibha Khare

MGM Medical College, Indore, Madhya Pradesh, India

Introduction: Thalassemia is a group of disorders due to hereditary single gene defect resulting in hemoglobin production defect. Thalassaemia is the world’s most prevalent genetic disorder. Globally, thalassemia is the most common hereditary hemoglobinopathy, and occurs in 4.4/10,000 live births. The main cause of mortality and morbidity in patients with β thalassaemia major is iron overload. This harmful iron over load results in many complications like growth retardation, delayed sexual maturation and later on involvement of liver, heart and endocrine glands. Hepcidin plays a vital role in iron homeostasis in humans, regulating iron absorption from the intestine and its recycling by macrophages. Hepcidin controls the dietary iron absorption and release of iron from macrophages. The present study aimed to determine and compare the serum level of hepcidin in thalassemia major patients with controls.

Materials and Methods: This was case control study conducted in the department of biochemistry after Ethical committee approval. 35 diagnosed patients of β-thalassemia major were taken as cases, and 35 healthy age and sex matched were included as controls after taking informed consent. Samples were taken for determination of serum Hepcidin levels along with serum iron, serum ferritin and total iron binding capacity.

Results: Serum hepcidin level was found to be reduced in patients of thalassemia major as compared to controls. Statistically significant difference between two groups was observed (P < 0.001).

Conclusion: Determination of hepcidin concentration is a useful indicator for high risk of iron toxicity in patients of beta thalassaemia.

Keywords: β-thalassaemia major, hepcidin, iron

Introduction: The corona virus pandemic (COVID-19) has affected the Transfusion and healthcare settings to extremes. It was fiendishly difficult to balance between demand and blood supply. It is foremost to identify donor attitude and fear factors hindering blood donation during COVID-19 pandemic.

Methods: A prospective longitudinal survey was done using Google Forms through) E-mails b) Instant messaging services like WhatsApp, telegram) Social networking sites (viz. Facebook; LinkedIn; Twitter) after obtaining the ethical clearance.

Sanitization of blood bank, provision of mask for blood donor and blood bank staff these measures should be implemented to augment blood donation during COVID-19 pandemic.

Conclusion: Patients with high pre transfusion INR were more benefitted with FFP. These observations may help us refine institutional policies and minimize inappropriate usage of FFP.

Ruchi Punamia, Meenu Bajpai, Ashish Maheshwari

Institute of Liver and Biliary Sciences, New Delhi, India

Introduction: Therapeutic plasma exchange (TPE) in liver disease patients is challenging due to ongoing coagulopathy, inflammation, and unstable hemodynamics. Adverse events (AE) in these specific patients have never been explored before. Thorough analysis is the need of the hour as TPE is indicated in a plethora of liver patients.

Aims: Analysis of Adverse Events associated with TPE with respect to nature, incidence, and possible risk factors for AE and suggest preventive strategies based on it.

Study Design and Methods: This was a retrospective observational study done in the super-specialty tertiary care center on 2470 TPE procedures in 936 patients with liver diseases in the last four years. We collected data on patient demographics, clinical indications, and laboratory parameters and analyzed them for any correlation with incidence, type (systemic, local, and device-related) and severity (mild, moderate, and severe) of AE recorded.

Results: In the study, AE were observed in 254 (10.3%) procedures. Most AEs were mild (46.06%), followed by moderate (38.18%) and severe (14.46%) based on their severity. Overall, 228 (89.06%) were systemic, 15 (5.85%) local and 13 (5.07%) device-related. Among the systemic AE, the most common were allergic AE 86 (26.7%), and hypotension 69 (21.43%). 37 (1.5%) procedures were aborted in the study period. Pediatric patients had a greater risk of AE (P = 0.026) than adults. Significant risk factors associated with AE were age (P = 0.020), need for vasopressor support (P = 0.001), and sepsis (P = 0.049). Low pre-TPE Mg levels (P = 0.001) and the amount of calcium infused during TPE (P = 0.002) significantly impacted AE.

Conclusion: Commonly encountered AE were systemic (allergic or hypotension) and mild in severity. These AE were manageable with strict monitoring and timely intervention. A meticulous hemovigilance reporting system, along with prospective studies for drafting policies and recommendations is needed.

Results: In forward group there was negative reaction with Anti A, Anti B and Anti AB sera and 4+ reaction was showing with Anti D sera. In reverse grouping there was 4+ reaction with A cell, B cell and O cell and negative reaction with Auto control. Patient was non-secretor for A, B and H antigen. Patient was diagnosed with probable Bombay phenotype (hh) and we tried to manage anemia with patient blood management. Patient was started on oral iron supplementation and after two month of treatment patient had Hb of 13.3 gm/dl with no complain of fatigue and weakness.

Discussion: We reiterate the importance of patient blood Management and would also like to emphasize on the need of the hour to implement the rare group registry in India under the direct supervision of a regulating organization like NBTC.

Conclusion: AEs in plateletphersis were mostly experienced by first time donors and donors who had previous history of some AEs. Post-donation telephonic interview proves to be efficient tool to collect information systematically.
